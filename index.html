The objective
What This Report Measures Against
“APA members are informed, engaged, and vote.”
APA Section 6 Negotiations Messaging Guide (APA_Messaging_Guide_7/27/26), July 2026

September had three moments that put APA in front of members and the public: the 25th anniversary of 9/11, Capt. Nick Silva’s public questioning of American’s leadership as Section 6 talks opened, and Senate passage of the pilot mental-health bills APA backed. APA’s leaders carried the coverage, and APA’s own channels drew their strongest response from member-life and remembrance content. The tone of public conversation about American’s pilots and their contract improved from August.

Cadence
How Often APA Published

APA’s website is its steadiest channel. 97 news articles went up on alliedpilots.org in September (111 in August), with something new on 27 of 30 days. Outside the 9/11 week, APA averaged about four social posts a week across all three platforms, and LinkedIn carried only two all month.

Section 6 read-across
What September Means for Negotiations

Each tab measures something different, but they all bear on one question: are members informed and engaged as Section 6 talks get under way? APA delivered its Section 6 openers in August, so September is the first full month of bargaining. On the evidence below, the public side is in better shape than the member side. Coverage and public tone held up, but the members who are paying closest attention are focused on scheduling and APU, doubt the process, and are getting less negotiation content than they did in August.

Watch list
Risks and Opportunities
Worth watching
Headline framing. The guide calls for a media tone that is “reasoned and methodical, not combative.” Coverage of the Sept 16 comments leaned the other way in its headlines.
The contract as a competitive issue. Two pieces argued the pilot contract is costing American in Austin, a framing that may resurface during talks.
Political giving. The APA PAC appeared in a story about donors to lawmakers who made remarks about Islam.
Pilot conduct stories. A viral jet bridge confrontation identified an American pilot by his APA lanyard.
Opportunities
The Senate mental-health win. Capt. Silva’s quote ran in 32 placements, and APA posted the press release on its website Sept 25, but it never went to Facebook, Instagram or LinkedIn.
Member-life content. It is APA’s most engaging material and a natural carrier for Section 6 updates.
October poll. This is the first read since the September 2025 baseline, when only 58% rated Board communication good, down from 77%. Repeating that question will show whether Section 6 communication is closing the gap.
Recommendations
What the Data Supports
01
Share the Senate bill win with members
The mental-health bills APA supported passed the Senate with Capt. Silva quoted in 32 placements, yet no APA post on Facebook, Instagram or LinkedIn covered them. A member-facing post or video message would turn earned coverage into member information.
02
Pair Section 6 updates with member-life content
Member-life posts drew about three times the engagement per post of Section 6 posts (1,952 vs. 648). Keep “Meet the Negotiators” running, and place negotiation updates alongside the events and people members respond to.
03
Give Pillar 3 (APU) a visible moment
Apart from the Sept 1 town hall clips, the accountability pillar had no measurable activity. A short update on APU fixes would give members something concrete, which the guide calls “actions speak louder than words.”
04
Check the leadership message against the guide’s media tone
Before the next statement on American’s leadership, weigh how September’s headlines read against the guide’s “reasoned, not combative” standard and its advice to let third parties carry the strongest lines.
05
Tighten monitoring before October
Most of what four Meltwater searches return is noise. For example, 212 of 214 “APU” results were unrelated. There is no ALPA volume search, and the listening topic still catches World War II “Allied pilots.” Fixing these makes next month’s trends reliable.
06
Close the data gaps
Set up GA4 key events (form submissions, downloads, video plays) so the site can show outcomes, connect Search Console, share the 2025 poll raw data and the October questionnaire, and have IT review the test sites in Google and the News page’s page-view tracking (SEO and Website tabs).

How Member Conversation Built, 2019–2023
Monthly posts in negotiation threads on The Line
Red bars: July 2019–August 2023 (63,570 posts from 1,664 pilots in 1,294 threads). Gray bar: September 2026, measured the same way. Hover a bar for the count of posts and pilots. The forum opened in July 2019, after talks were already under way, so the start of the last round isn’t captured.

For three years the conversation ran at a few hundred to about 1,000 posts a month. It jumped when there was something concrete to react to. The October 2022 tentative agreement, which the Board rejected on Nov 2, drew 5,905 posts. The May 2023 Agreement in Principle drew 7,244. July 2023, when a TA went to members, the vote was postponed days after United’s pilots reached their own deal, and TA 2.0 followed, drew 9,169 posts from 761 pilots. That was the busiest month of the round.

Lessons
What Last Round Tells Us About This One
01
The forum reads more negative than the membership
At the end, the forum’s exit poll had 56% yes among pilots who said how they voted. The real result was 72.7% yes. Earlier, TA 1.0 polled 85% no on the forum. Use the forum to see which issues are live, not to forecast a vote.
02
Trust in the process was the steadiest theme
Posts about the Negotiating Committee, the Board and union leadership ran at 12–18% of negotiation posts for most of the round, about as often as pay. In September 2026 they are 23%, higher than in any phase of the last round.
03
Solidarity fights come back
About 3,400 posts last round argued over pilots, and union leaders, picking up premium flying while APA sought leverage. APU is this round’s version of that fight.
04
Peer deals set expectations
Mentions of Delta and United climbed once their deals landed, and United’s July 2023 agreement led to the postponed vote and TA 2.0. Expect the next Delta or United contract to reset what members see as good enough.
05
Peaks follow documents
The three busiest months each followed something members could read: a TA, an AIP, a revised TA. Communication plans should be ready before each one lands, not after.

01

Confidence in the Negotiating Committee is low among posters
A 71-post thread argued the Negotiating Committee and JIAC should be returned to the line. 03
The Sept 18 bargaining update drew skepticism, but also ideas
The Negotiating Committee’s update on initial bargaining and the NMB was the most-read APA communication on the forum. Reactions called it light on detail. 04
The CEO critique landed with mixed reviews

A senior-versus-junior divide runs through everything
Senior posters say APA works for the junior majority and discounts their history, including in replies to the President’s Labor Day message. Takeaways
What the Survey Tells Us
01
Members value APA, but feel less informed by the Board
81% said APA’s services and representation are a very good or good value. But only 58% rated the Board of Directors’ communication and keeping members informed as very good or good, down 19 points from 77% in February 2025. That rating fell in every age, base, seat and seniority group UNH reported. It is the survey’s most direct measure of the “informed” half of APA’s objective.
02
Overall sentiment is recovering but hasn’t returned to 2024 levels
51% viewed APA very positively or positively, up from 48% in February 2025 but below 60% in January 2024. 18% were negative. Among negative respondents, the leading reasons were poor contract negotiations (23%), “corruption and self-serving leadership” (19%) and lack of representation and listening to members (15%). Among positive respondents, the top reason was the union’s focus on pilot interests (21%).
03
Scheduling, not pay rates, is the top contract priority
60% named scheduling rules as the most important thing to improve in bargaining, and 80% ranked it first or second. Compensation was first for 18% (46% first or second). Within scheduling, improved pairing quality led at 47%. Within pay, trip and duty rigs led at 38% (61% first or second), ahead of hourly rates (26%).
04
Members want a better deal over a faster one
55% disagreed that it is better to reach a deal sooner with fewer changes (29% agreed). 73% disagreed with securing a contract first so APA can re-enter talks sooner “even if it means we set the market,” and 61% agreed it is better to let other carriers ratify first. Junior pilots hold this view most strongly: 76% of 31-to-40-year-olds rejected a faster, smaller deal, compared with 36% of pilots 61 and older.
05
Support for “spreading the wealth” on premium flying is rising
64% agreed with scheduling changes that would “spread the wealth,” such as Seniority in Turn for Premium, up from 49% in September 2024. Agreement that the most senior pilots should always get first opportunity at premium sequences slipped from 59% to 53%. This is the fault line behind Aggressive Pick-Up, and it splits by seniority.

Data quality
Read These Numbers With Care
01
August includes traffic that doesn’t look like members
Several August landing pages drew one-page, near-zero-engagement visits: News (8,690 sessions, 20% engaged), Spouse Support Network (1,802, 7%) and Careers (1,467, 5%). August also logged 13,724 form starts from 13,726 users, compared with 1,750 in September. That pattern suggests automated or accidental traffic, but GA4 alone can’t confirm it. The engaged-session count, down 17% (60,513 vs. 73,071), is a fairer comparison than raw sessions.
02
Page views are inflated by the News page
Visits that start on the News page logged 137,987 page views in September, about 40 per visit with a 28-minute average. That is 36% of all page views on the site, which points to an auto-refresh or tracking issue. Page views are left out of this report until IT checks it.
03
Two other GA4 properties are connected
AgencyAnalytics also has two small properties (288 and 255 sessions in September) with the same page structure as alliedpilots.org. They may be the test sites found in Google (SEO tab), but that isn’t confirmed. They are excluded here. 6,240 sessions with no landing page recorded, at 3% engagement, are included in the totals.
